# Hand Hygiene Policy for Clinical Staff (Revised)

## Policy Statement and Changes

All staff working in patient-care and clinical research areas must perform hand hygiene in accordance with this policy. This revision replaces the 2018 policy. The minimum handrub time increases from 15 seconds to 20–30 seconds, the approved alcohol concentration is narrowed to 60–80% to match the formulation stocked by the hospital, and monitoring moves from monthly manager audits to continuous observation by trained, independent observers.

## Approved Products and Method

Alcohol-based handrub containing 60–80% alcohol is the approved method for routine hand antisepsis. Dispense enough product to cover all hand surfaces and rub palms, backs of hands, between fingers, thumbs and fingertips for 20–30 seconds until the hands are dry. Use soap and water, washing for 40–60 seconds, when hands are visibly soiled, after using the toilet, and when caring for patients on enteric precautions, such as those with Clostridioides difficile or norovirus infection.

## Required Opportunities

Hand hygiene is required on entry to and exit from every patient room; before touching a patient; before any clean or aseptic procedure, including handling of central lines, research specimens or investigational products; after any body fluid exposure risk; after touching a patient; and after touching the patient's surroundings. Gloves never replace hand hygiene, and hand hygiene is required immediately after glove removal.

## Research Units

Staff in clinical research units must also perform hand hygiene before handling investigational products and before entering clean preparation rooms. Visiting investigators and study monitors follow the same requirements as hospital staff while in patient-care areas.

## Monitoring and Accountability

Trained observers who are independent of the unit measure compliance continuously, and units receive weekly dashboards. Units below 90% compliance for two consecutive months submit an improvement plan to the hospital epidemiology service. Repeated individual non-compliance is managed through just-culture coaching before any disciplinary process.
