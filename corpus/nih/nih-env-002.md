# Patient Room Cleaning Procedure for Environmental Services

## Daily Cleaning

Occupied rooms are cleaned every day. High-touch surfaces, including bed rails, overbed tables, call buttons, door handles, light switches and bathroom fixtures, are cleaned and disinfected at least daily and whenever visibly soiled, and more often in intensive care and contact precautions rooms. Work from clean to dirty and from high to low, and use a fresh wipe for each surface or item.

## Products and Wet Contact Time

Remove visible soil before disinfecting. Use only the hospital's approved products and keep the surface wet for the full contact time: 2 minutes for quaternary ammonium-alcohol wipes, 1 minute for accelerated hydrogen peroxide, and 4 minutes for 1,000 ppm chlorine in Clostridioides difficile and norovirus rooms. Reapply if the surface dries early.

## Blood and Body Fluid Spills

Wear gloves, absorb the spill with disposable towels, clean the area, and then disinfect it with a 5,000 ppm chlorine solution for 10 minutes. Discard the waste as regulated medical waste.

## Chemical Safety

Never mix chlorine products with acids or ammonia-based cleaners, because toxic gases are released. Keep products in their original labeled containers and wear eye protection when splashes are possible.

## Discharge Cleaning and Audits

After discharge or transfer, perform a terminal clean of all surfaces and equipment and change privacy curtains. Supervisors audit cleaning with fluorescent gel markers and ATP testing and give feedback to staff.
