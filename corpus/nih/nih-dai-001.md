# Prevention of Ventilator-Associated Events

## Background

Ventilator-associated events, including ventilator-associated pneumonia, are among the most serious complications of mechanical ventilation and are associated with longer ventilation, longer stays in intensive care and higher mortality. The most effective prevention strategy is to minimize the duration of mechanical ventilation, supported by measures that reduce aspiration of contaminated secretions.

## Ventilator Bundle

1. Elevate the head of the bed to 30–45 degrees unless contraindicated.
2. Perform a daily sedation interruption, or use light sedation targets, combined with a daily spontaneous breathing trial.
3. Use endotracheal tubes with subglottic secretion drainage for patients expected to require ventilation for more than 48–72 hours.
4. Maintain endotracheal cuff pressure between 20 and 30 cm H2O.
5. Provide oral care with toothbrushing at least twice daily.
6. Encourage early mobilization.

## Equipment Management

Change ventilator circuits only when visibly soiled or malfunctioning, not on a fixed schedule. Drain condensate away from the patient and avoid disconnecting the circuit unnecessarily. Use closed suction systems where available, and perform hand hygiene and wear gloves before handling the airway.

## Avoiding Intubation

Use non-invasive ventilation or high-flow nasal oxygen for selected patients when it is safe to do so, because avoiding intubation eliminates the risk of ventilator-associated events.

## Surveillance and Review

Ventilator-associated events are tracked per 1,000 ventilator days using standard surveillance definitions. Bundle compliance is audited on daily rounds, and the intensive care quality committee reviews both outcome and process data every month.
