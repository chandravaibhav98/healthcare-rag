# Surgical Instrument Reprocessing Workflow

## Point-of-Use Treatment

Immediately after a procedure, the clinical team removes gross soil from instruments with a sterile sponge and water, opens hinged instruments, and keeps them moist using an enzymatic foam or a moist towel. Sharps are removed and disposed of by the user. Instruments are placed in a closed, labeled, rigid container for transport to sterile processing, and must arrive within the time limit set by sterile processing so that soil does not dry.

## Cleaning and Inspection

In the decontamination area, staff wearing fluid-resistant gowns, gloves, and face protection disassemble instruments according to the manufacturer's instructions for use and clean them manually or in an automated washer-disinfector. Lumens are brushed with appropriately sized brushes and flushed. After cleaning, each instrument is inspected under lighted magnification for residual soil, damage and corrosion, and tested for function. Instruments that fail inspection are recleaned or removed from service.

## Packaging and Loading

Clean instruments are assembled into sets using a count sheet and packaged in wrap or rigid containers that allow sterilant penetration. An internal chemical indicator is placed in every set. Sets are loaded so that steam can circulate freely: containers flat, wrapped packs on edge, and nothing touching the chamber walls. Heavy sets should not exceed 11.3 kilograms.

## Sterilization and Release

Sets are processed using the validated cycle for the contents, typically a pre-vacuum steam cycle at 132–135 °C. The operator reviews the printout and external indicators before release. Loads containing implants are quarantined until the biological indicator result is negative. Each set is labeled with a load number that links it to the cycle record and, after use, to the patient record.

## Immediate-Use Steam Sterilization

Immediate-use steam sterilization is reserved for urgent situations when no other option exists, and must not be used for implants or for convenience. Every instance is logged and reviewed by the sterile processing manager to identify inventory shortages.
