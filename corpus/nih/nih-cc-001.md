# Vaccine Temperature Monitoring and Excursion Response

## Responsibilities

The pharmacy department owns all vaccine storage units in the hospital. Each unit has a named primary and backup vaccine coordinator who is trained annually in storage, monitoring and excursion response. Clinical units may hold vaccines only in pharmacy-approved storage units connected to the central monitoring system.

## Continuous Monitoring

Every vaccine refrigerator and freezer is connected to the hospital's continuous temperature monitoring system, which records the temperature every 5 minutes through a buffered probe and sends an alarm to the pharmacy and to facilities engineering when the temperature leaves the range of 2–8 °C for refrigerators or −50 °C to −15 °C for freezers. The vaccine coordinator reviews the temperature history at the start of each workday and documents the review in the monitoring system.

## Alarm Response

When an alarm sounds, the responder checks the unit within 15 minutes, confirms the reading with the display, and looks for a cause such as an open door or power failure. If the temperature cannot be restored promptly, vaccines are moved to a backup unit using qualified transport containers.

## Excursion Handling

1. Label affected vaccines "do not use" and keep them at 2–8 °C or in the freezer range as appropriate.
2. Notify the vaccine coordinator and the pharmacy manager immediately.
3. Export the temperature record for the excursion, including maximum or minimum temperature and duration.
4. Contact the manufacturer and the immunization program for a viability determination.
5. Return vaccines to use or dispose of them only after the written determination is received.

## Review and Improvement

Every excursion is entered in the pharmacy quality database. Recurrent excursions in the same unit trigger an engineering review and, if necessary, replacement of the unit.
