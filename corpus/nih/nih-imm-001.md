# Occupational Immunization and Documentation Requirements

## Requirements at Hire

Before starting work in patient-care or clinical research areas, every staff member must provide documentation of immunity to measles, mumps, rubella and varicella, and must have received or declined hepatitis B vaccination if their duties involve exposure to blood. Staff without evidence of immunity are vaccinated by occupational medicine at no cost. Clearance to start work in clinical areas is withheld until requirements are met or a medical exemption is approved.

## Annual Requirements

All staff in patient-care and clinical research areas receive influenza vaccine every year, by the date announced at the start of the influenza season. Staff with an approved medical or religious exemption wear a medical mask in patient-care areas for the duration of the influenza season. COVID-19 vaccination is offered according to the current national recommendations.

## Hepatitis B

The hepatitis B series is given according to the product schedule, commonly three doses at 0, 1 and 6 months. Anti-HBs is measured 1–2 months after the final dose. A result of at least 10 mIU/mL is documented as immune; staff with a lower result receive a second series and are retested. Immune staff do not need booster doses or periodic retesting.

## Pertussis and Tetanus

Staff receive one dose of Tdap if they have not previously received it as an adult, regardless of the interval since the last tetanus-containing vaccine, followed by Td or Tdap boosters every 10 years. Staff working in neonatal and obstetric units are prioritized.

## Records and Privacy

Immunization records are stored in the confidential occupational health record, not in the personnel file. Managers receive only a compliant or non-compliant status for their staff. Records are retained for the duration of employment plus 30 years where they relate to exposure management.
