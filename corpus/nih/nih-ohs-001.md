# Occupational Health Screening of Clinical Staff

## Pre-Placement Evaluation

Every new staff member who will work in patient-care or clinical research areas completes a pre-placement evaluation with occupational medicine before starting work. The evaluation reviews immunization status, tuberculosis screening, respirator medical clearance where required, and any conditions that may require work accommodations. The evaluation is confidential and is not used to make employment decisions unrelated to job safety.

## Tuberculosis Screening

At hire, staff receive baseline tuberculosis screening that includes a symptom questionnaire, an individual risk assessment and a test for latent infection, using an interferon-gamma release assay or a tuberculin skin test. Staff with a positive test have a chest radiograph and clinical evaluation and are offered treatment for latent infection. After hire, staff complete an annual tuberculosis education module and symptom review; routine serial testing is not performed unless there is a known exposure or the worker is in a designated higher-risk role.

## Work Restrictions for Illness

Staff with fever, acute respiratory symptoms, vomiting or diarrhoea, draining skin lesions or conjunctivitis must not work in patient-care areas and must report to occupational medicine. Staff with norovirus-like illness remain off work until 48 hours after their symptoms resolve. Staff with influenza-like illness remain off work until they have been without fever for at least 24 hours without antipyretic medicine. Herpes zoster lesions must be covered, and staff with localized zoster do not care for high-risk patients until the lesions are crusted.

## Exposure Follow-Up

After an exposure to a communicable disease, occupational medicine identifies exposed staff, checks their immunity, and determines whether post-exposure prophylaxis or work exclusion is needed. Non-immune staff exposed to measles or varicella are excluded from work from the 5th day after the first exposure through the 21st day after the last exposure.

## Confidentiality

Occupational health records are kept separate from personnel records, and information is shared with managers only to the extent needed to apply work restrictions.
