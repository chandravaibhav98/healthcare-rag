# Isolation Starting, Stopping and Transport Procedure

## Starting Isolation

Start precautions as soon as an infection is suspected, without waiting for laboratory results. Contact precautions require a gown and gloves at room entry. Droplet precautions require a mask within close range and at least 1 metre between beds. Airborne precautions require an isolation room with negative pressure and 12 air changes per hour, and an N95 respirator or PAPR for staff.

## Stopping Isolation

Hospital epidemiology, the hospital's infection prevention and control service, decides when precautions can be discontinued and documents the decision in the medical record. For suspected tuberculosis, airborne isolation continues until three sputum smear or molecular tests collected at least 8 hours apart, including one early-morning specimen, are negative, or another diagnosis is confirmed.

## Transport

Patients leave the isolation room only for essential tests or procedures. They wear a surgical mask during transport for droplet and airborne precautions, infected areas are covered, and the receiving department is told in advance.

## Visitors

Visitors are limited, taught the precautions in use and offered the recommended PPE.
