# Central Line and Urinary Catheter Care Procedure

## Central Line Insertion

Central lines are inserted using the hospital insertion checklist, and the observer may stop the procedure if asepsis is broken. The operator performs hand hygiene and uses maximal sterile barrier precautions: cap, mask, sterile gown, sterile gloves and a full-body sterile drape. The skin is prepared with alcoholic chlorhexidine containing more than 0.5% chlorhexidine and allowed to dry. The femoral site is avoided in adults, and ultrasound guidance is used.

## Central Line Maintenance

Before every access, staff perform hand hygiene and scrub the hub or needleless connector with an antiseptic. Transparent dressings are changed at least every 7 days and gauze dressings every 2 days, or immediately if damp, loose or soiled. Administration sets not used for blood or lipids are replaced no more often than every 96 hours and at least every 7 days. The need for the line is reviewed on daily rounds and nonessential lines are removed.

## Urinary Catheter Indications and Insertion

Indwelling urinary catheters are placed only for approved indications, such as acute urinary retention, selected perioperative use or accurate output monitoring in critically ill patients, and never for staff convenience. They are inserted with aseptic technique and sterile equipment.

## Urinary Catheter Maintenance and Removal

Keep the drainage system closed, keep urine flowing, and keep the collection bag below the level of the bladder and off the floor. Empty the bag with a clean container for each patient. Nurses remove catheters under the nurse-driven removal protocol as soon as they are no longer needed, and external or intermittent catheterization is considered as an alternative.

## Measurement

Bloodstream and urinary tract infection rates are reported per 1,000 central line days and per 1,000 catheter days, with insertion checklist compliance, and shared with unit teams each month.
