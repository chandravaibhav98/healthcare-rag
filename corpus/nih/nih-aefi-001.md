# Clinical Assessment and Reporting of Adverse Events After Immunization

## Common Expected Reactions

Most reactions after vaccination are mild and resolve within 1–3 days. They include pain, redness and swelling at the injection site, low-grade fever, headache, fatigue and muscle aches. Staff should explain these expected reactions to vaccine recipients before vaccination and advise them how to manage discomfort and when to seek care.

## Immediate Reactions in the Clinic

Recipients are observed for 15 minutes after vaccination, or 30 minutes if they have a history of severe allergic reaction or anaphylaxis. Staff distinguish vasovagal syncope, which typically presents with pallor, sweating and a slow pulse, from anaphylaxis, which presents with rapid onset of skin, respiratory or cardiovascular signs. Anaphylaxis is treated immediately with intramuscular epinephrine according to the hospital emergency protocol, and the emergency team is called.

## Assessment of a Reported Event

When a recipient reports an adverse event after leaving the clinic, the clinician documents the onset time, the symptoms, their severity and duration, any treatment given and the outcome. The vaccine name, manufacturer, lot number, dose number and injection site are taken from the immunization record. The clinician assesses whether the event is serious and whether it could be explained by another cause.

## Internal and External Reporting

All clinically significant events are entered in the hospital safety reporting system and reported to the national vaccine adverse event reporting system. Serious events, including any anaphylaxis, hospitalization or death, are also reported immediately by telephone to occupational medicine for staff vaccinees and to hospital epidemiology. Immunization errors are reported even when no harm occurred.

## Follow-Up of Recipients

Staff who experience a serious reaction are evaluated by occupational medicine before any further dose of the same vaccine, and a precaution or contraindication is recorded in their immunization record when appropriate.
