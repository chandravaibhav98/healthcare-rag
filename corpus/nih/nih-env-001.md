# Disinfectant Selection and Contact Times

## Purpose

This procedure tells environmental services, nursing and research staff which disinfectants to use on environmental surfaces and non-critical equipment, and how long each must remain wet to be effective. Using the wrong product or wiping a surface dry too early is a frequent cause of cleaning failure.

## Approved Product Classes

| Product class | Typical use | Wet contact time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quaternary ammonium with alcohol wipes | Routine disinfection of equipment and high-touch surfaces | 2 minutes |
| Accelerated hydrogen peroxide 0.5% | Patient rooms and shared equipment | 1 minute |
| Chlorine-releasing agent 1,000 ppm | Clostridioides difficile and norovirus rooms | 4 minutes |
| Chlorine-releasing agent 5,000 ppm | Blood spills after removal of visible material | 10 minutes |

Contact times in this table are examples for the products stocked by the hospital; always follow the label of the product in use.

## How to Achieve the Contact Time

Remove visible soil first. Apply the disinfectant liberally so that the surface stays visibly wet for the full contact time, using additional wipes on large or porous surfaces. Do not dry the surface before the contact time has elapsed. One wipe should be used for one area or item and then discarded, to avoid spreading contamination.

## Compatibility and Safety

Check that the product is compatible with the equipment manufacturer's instructions, because some disinfectants damage screens, plastics and seals. Wear gloves when using disinfectants and eye protection when splashes are possible. Never mix chlorine products with acids or ammonia. Store products in their original labeled containers.

## Research Laboratories

Biological safety cabinets and laboratory benches are disinfected with products validated against the agents in use, following the laboratory's biosafety plan, which takes precedence over this procedure for laboratory areas.
