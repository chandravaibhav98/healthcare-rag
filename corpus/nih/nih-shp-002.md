# Needlestick and Splash Exposure Procedure

## Which Exposures Count

Report any percutaneous injury, such as a needlestick or a cut from a contaminated sharp, and any splash of blood or body fluids onto the eyes, nose, mouth or non-intact skin. Contact of blood with intact skin is not an exposure.

## First Aid

Wash the wound or skin with soap and water and flush the eyes, nose or mouth with water or saline. Do not squeeze the wound and do not apply bleach or other caustic agents.

## Report Immediately

Report the exposure to your supervisor and to occupational medicine at once, using the emergency page outside office hours, and complete the online injury report before the end of the shift.

## Source Testing

With consent, the source patient is tested for hepatitis B surface antigen, hepatitis C antibody and HIV, using a rapid HIV test so that decisions are not delayed.

## Post-Exposure Prophylaxis

When indicated, HIV post-exposure prophylaxis starts as soon as possible, ideally within 2 hours and no later than 72 hours, and is taken for 4 weeks. Non-immune staff exposed to a hepatitis B positive or unknown source receive hepatitis B immune globulin and vaccine. There is no post-exposure prophylaxis for hepatitis C; staff are followed up with testing so that infection can be treated early.
