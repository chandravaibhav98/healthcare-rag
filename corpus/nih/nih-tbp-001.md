# Management of Patients Requiring Airborne Isolation

## Indications

Airborne isolation is required for patients with known or suspected pulmonary or laryngeal tuberculosis, measles, varicella, disseminated herpes zoster, and for patients undergoing aerosol-generating procedures when infected with a novel respiratory pathogen designated by hospital epidemiology. Isolation is started at the time of suspicion, without waiting for laboratory confirmation.

## Airborne Infection Isolation Rooms

Patients are placed in an airborne infection isolation room with continuous negative pressure, at least 12 air changes per hour and exhaust air vented outside or through HEPA filtration. Facilities engineering verifies negative pressure daily when the room is occupied, using the room pressure monitor or a smoke test, and records the result. The door is kept closed except for entry and exit, and an anteroom is used where available.

## Staff Protection

All staff entering the room wear a fit-tested N95 respirator or a PAPR. Only staff with documented immunity to measles and varicella enter the rooms of patients with these infections when immune staff are available. Pregnant staff are informed of the risks so that they can discuss assignments with their supervisor.

## Tuberculosis-Specific Measures

Patients with suspected tuberculosis remain in airborne isolation until tuberculosis is excluded, usually after three negative sputum smear or molecular test results collected at least 8 hours apart, including at least one early-morning specimen, or until an alternative diagnosis is confirmed. Patients with confirmed tuberculosis remain isolated until they are on effective therapy, show clinical improvement and have three consecutive negative sputum smears, as determined by hospital epidemiology.

## Patient Movement and Visitors

Patients leave the room only for essential procedures, wearing a surgical mask, and the receiving department is notified in advance. Visitors are limited to essential persons, receive instructions on precautions and wear the protection recommended by hospital epidemiology.
