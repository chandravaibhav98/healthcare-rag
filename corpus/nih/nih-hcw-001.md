# Segregation of Laboratory, Chemical and Pharmaceutical Waste

## Principles

Waste from clinical research laboratories and pharmacies is often hazardous for more than one reason, for example an infectious specimen preserved in a chemical fixative. Staff classify such mixed waste by its most hazardous component and consult the waste management office when unsure. Segregation is performed at the point of generation, and waste is never moved between containers after it has been placed in them.

## Biological Laboratory Waste

Cultures, stocks and other materials containing infectious agents are decontaminated by autoclaving within the laboratory or placed in red biohazard bags inside rigid, leak-proof secondary containers for collection. Autoclave bags are not overfilled, and indicator tape is used on every load. Liquid biological waste is decontaminated chemically or by autoclaving before drain disposal, as specified in the laboratory's biosafety plan.

## Chemical Waste

Chemical waste is collected in compatible, closed containers labeled with the words "hazardous waste", the full chemical names and the accumulation start date. Incompatible chemicals, such as acids and bases or oxidizers and organics, are stored separately. Containers are kept in a designated satellite accumulation area at or near the point of generation, and full containers are collected within 3 days.

## Pharmaceutical and Hazardous Drug Waste

Expired or unused medications are returned to pharmacy and never disposed of in drains or general waste. Trace-contaminated items from hazardous drug administration, such as empty vials, gloves and gowns, go into yellow trace chemotherapy containers, while bulk hazardous drug waste goes into black hazardous waste containers. Controlled substances are wasted with a witness and documented according to pharmacy policy.

## Training and Inspection

Staff who generate hazardous waste complete waste management training before working in the area and refresher training every year. The waste management office inspects satellite accumulation areas monthly and reports deficiencies to the laboratory chief.
