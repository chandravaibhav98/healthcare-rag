# Investigating Clusters of Health Care-Associated Infections

## Detecting a Cluster

A cluster is an aggregation of cases of infection or colonization that are closely grouped in time or place and that may be greater than the number expected. Clusters are detected through routine surveillance, review of microbiology reports, unusual antimicrobial susceptibility patterns and reports from frontline staff. A cluster is not necessarily an outbreak, but every cluster deserves a prompt initial assessment by the infection prevention team.

## Initial Assessment

Verify that the cases are real, for example by excluding laboratory error, pseudo-outbreaks caused by specimen contamination, or changes in testing methods or case finding. Compare the current number of cases with the baseline rate for the same unit and season. Report the cluster to the facility leadership and, when required, to the state or local health department, which can provide epidemiological and laboratory support.

## Case Definition and Line List

Create a working case definition that specifies the clinical or laboratory criteria, the location and the time period. Start broad to capture possible cases and refine the definition as information accrues. Record each case in a line list with patient identifiers, dates of admission and onset, location history, procedures, devices, exposures and laboratory results. Use the line list to draw an epidemic curve and a map of cases by unit and room.

## Infection Control Assessment

Observe practices in the affected area, including hand hygiene, use of PPE, injection practices, device insertion and maintenance, and cleaning and disinfection of shared equipment. Review recent changes in products, staff, procedures or construction. Laboratory typing of isolates, such as whole-genome sequencing, helps determine whether cases are related.

## Interventions and Follow-Up

Implement control measures based on the suspected mode of transmission without waiting for laboratory confirmation. Communicate regularly with staff, patients and leadership. Continue surveillance after interventions to confirm that transmission has stopped, and document the investigation, findings and corrective actions in a final report.
