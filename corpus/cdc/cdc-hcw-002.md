# Regulated Medical Waste Quick Reference

## What Is Regulated Medical Waste

Regulated medical waste, also called infectious waste, includes microbiology cultures and stocks, pathology and anatomy waste, blood and blood products, and sharps. Items that are not saturated with blood, such as most soiled dressings, are usually general waste. Classifying ordinary waste as regulated medical waste adds cost without adding safety.

## Bags and Containers

Use red bags or containers with the biohazard symbol for regulated medical waste. Put sharps in closable, puncture-resistant, leak-proof sharps containers and replace them before they overfill, usually when they are three-quarters full. Close every container before it is moved.

## Spills

Clean blood and body fluid spills promptly. Wear gloves, absorb the spill with disposable towels, clean the area, and disinfect it with an EPA-registered disinfectant or a fresh bleach dilution for the full contact time. Discard the materials as regulated medical waste.

## Storage, Transport and Treatment

Store regulated medical waste in a secure area with limited access, protected from weather and pests. Move it in closed carts. It is treated by steam sterilization, incineration or another state-approved method before disposal, and licensed haulers carry it off site with a tracking manifest.
