# Preventing Central Line-Associated Bloodstream and Catheter-Associated Urinary Tract Infections

## CLABSI Prevention at Insertion

Central line-associated bloodstream infections (CLABSI) are largely preventable. At insertion, HCP must perform hand hygiene, use maximal sterile barrier precautions including a cap, mask, sterile gown, sterile gloves and a sterile full-body drape, and prepare clean skin with a chlorhexidine preparation with alcohol containing more than 0.5% chlorhexidine. Avoid the femoral vein for central venous access in adult patients, and use an insertion checklist with authority for the observer to stop the procedure if aseptic technique is broken.

## CLABSI Prevention During Maintenance

Scrub the access port or hub with an appropriate antiseptic such as chlorhexidine, povidone-iodine or 70% alcohol, and access it only with sterile devices. Change gauze dressings every 2 days and transparent dressings at least every 7 days, and replace any dressing that is damp, loose or visibly soiled. Replace administration sets not used for blood or lipids no more frequently than every 96 hours but at least every 7 days. Assess the need for the central line every day and remove nonessential lines promptly.

## CAUTI Prevention

Catheter-associated urinary tract infections (CAUTI) are prevented first by avoiding unnecessary catheters. Insert urinary catheters only for appropriate indications, such as acute urinary retention, perioperative use for selected surgical procedures, or the need for accurate output measurement in critically ill patients. Use aseptic technique and sterile equipment for insertion. Maintain a sterile, continuously closed drainage system, keep the collecting bag below the level of the bladder at all times and not on the floor, and empty it regularly using a separate collecting container for each patient.

## Removal Reminders and Alternatives

Use reminder systems or nurse-driven protocols to prompt removal of urinary catheters that are no longer needed. Consider alternatives to indwelling catheters, such as external catheters in men or intermittent catheterization, where appropriate.

## Measurement

Track CLABSI and CAUTI rates per 1,000 device days and device utilization ratios, and share them with frontline teams together with process measures such as insertion checklist compliance.
