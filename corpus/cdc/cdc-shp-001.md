# Occupational Exposure to Bloodborne Pathogens

## Exposures That Put HCP at Risk

An exposure that might place HCP at risk for hepatitis B, hepatitis C or HIV infection is defined as a percutaneous injury, such as a needlestick or cut with a sharp object, or contact of mucous membranes or non-intact skin with blood, tissue or other potentially infectious body fluids. Intact skin contact with blood is not considered an exposure. Percutaneous injuries with hollow-bore, blood-filled needles carry the highest risk.

## First Aid

Wash wounds and skin sites that have been in contact with blood or body fluids with soap and water. Flush mucous membranes with water. There is no evidence that expressing fluid by squeezing the wound reduces the risk of transmission, and the use of antiseptics or caustic agents such as bleach is not recommended. After first aid, the exposed person must report the incident immediately so that evaluation is not delayed.

## Evaluation of the Exposure and the Source

Occupational health evaluates the exposure for its potential to transmit infection, based on the type of body fluid and the route and severity of the exposure. The source patient is tested for hepatitis B surface antigen, hepatitis C antibody and HIV antibody, with informed consent according to local law. Rapid HIV testing of the source is preferred so that decisions about prophylaxis can be made quickly.

## HIV Post-Exposure Prophylaxis

When indicated, HIV post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) should be initiated as soon as possible, preferably within hours of the exposure rather than days. PEP is given for 4 weeks. The exposed person is re-evaluated within 72 hours as additional information becomes available, and follow-up HIV testing is performed at 6 weeks and 4 months, or at 6 weeks and 3 months if a newer combination antigen/antibody test is used.

## Hepatitis B and C Management

Post-exposure management for hepatitis B depends on the vaccination and antibody response status of the exposed person and the source's surface antigen status. Unvaccinated persons exposed to a positive or unknown source receive hepatitis B immune globulin and start the vaccine series. There is no post-exposure prophylaxis for hepatitis C; exposed HCP are tested to allow early diagnosis and treatment.
