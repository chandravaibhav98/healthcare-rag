# Cleaning and Disinfection of Patient-Care Areas

## Environmental Services Program

Every facility must have an environmental services program with written policies, defined responsibilities and trained staff. The program specifies which surfaces are cleaned, by whom, how often and with which products. Responsibilities for cleaning shared patient-care equipment are clearly assigned between nursing and environmental services so that no item is missed.

## Cleaning Before Disinfection

Cleaning is the removal of visible soil, organic and inorganic material, from objects and surfaces, and is normally accomplished manually or mechanically using water with detergents. Thorough cleaning is essential before disinfection, because inorganic and organic materials that remain on surfaces interfere with the effectiveness of the disinfection process. When a combined cleaner-disinfector is used, apply it once to clean and a second time to disinfect.

## High-Touch Surfaces

High-touch surfaces in patient rooms, including bed rails, overbed tables, call buttons, telephones, door knobs, light switches and bathroom fixtures, are cleaned and disinfected at least daily and when visibly soiled. In intensive care units and rooms of patients on contact precautions, more frequent cleaning is recommended. Clean from cleaner areas to dirtier areas and from high to low.

## Disinfectant Use and Contact Time

Use an EPA-registered hospital disinfectant appropriate for the pathogens of concern, following the label instructions for dilution, application and contact time. The contact time is the period the surface must stay wet for the disinfectant to be effective, commonly between 1 and 10 minutes depending on the product. If the surface dries before the contact time is reached, reapply the product. For rooms of patients with Clostridioides difficile, use a product with a sporicidal claim.

## Discharge Cleaning and Monitoring

After patient discharge or transfer, perform a thorough terminal clean of all surfaces and equipment, change privacy curtains when visibly soiled and on the schedule set by policy, and discard single-patient items. Monitor cleaning thoroughness using direct observation, fluorescent gel markers or ATP bioluminescence, and give feedback to staff and supervisors.
