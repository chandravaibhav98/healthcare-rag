# Vaccine Storage and Handling (Revised)

## Summary of Changes

This edition replaces version 3.0. Continuous temperature monitoring with a digital data logger is now required for every storage unit, replacing twice-daily manual readings as the primary method. Temperature excursions must now be reported to the immunization program immediately, rather than within 24 hours. Data logger records must be reviewed at least once each workday, and temperature records must be kept for at least 3 years.

## Storage Units and Placement

Use purpose-built or pharmaceutical-grade refrigerators and freezers for vaccine storage. Household combination units are permitted only for temporary refrigerated storage if the freezer compartment is never used for vaccines. Keep vaccines in their original packaging, in the center of the unit, with space for air circulation, and never in doors, drawers or bins. Store vaccines with the earliest expiration dates at the front.

## Temperature Ranges

Refrigerated vaccines must be stored between 2 °C and 8 °C, aiming for 5 °C. Frozen vaccines must be stored between −50 °C and −15 °C. Some vaccines require ultra-cold storage as specified by the manufacturer. Freezing destroys the potency of many refrigerated vaccines, so units must not be set close to 0 °C.

## Continuous Temperature Monitoring

Each storage unit must have a digital data logger with a buffered probe, a current certificate of calibration, an alarm for out-of-range temperatures and a display of current, minimum and maximum temperatures. Review the logger data and the minimum and maximum readings at least once each workday, preferably at the start of the day, and document the review. Download and keep the data for at least 3 years.

## Responding to Temperature Excursions

1. Label exposed vaccines "do not use" and keep them at the correct temperature; do not discard them.
2. Notify the vaccine coordinator and report the excursion to the immunization program immediately.
3. Record the date, time, duration and minimum or maximum temperature of the excursion.
4. Contact the manufacturer for viability guidance and follow the documented decision.
5. Correct the cause and document the corrective action.

## Emergency Vaccine Transport

Maintain a written emergency plan, qualified transport containers and a digital data logger for transport. Transport vaccines only when necessary, and record temperatures during transport.
