# Reporting Adverse Events After Vaccination

## Why Report

The national vaccine adverse event reporting system is an early warning system that detects possible safety problems after vaccines are licensed. Health care providers are encouraged to report any clinically significant adverse event that occurs after vaccination, even if they are not sure whether the vaccine caused it. Reports are used to identify unusual patterns that are then studied with more rigorous methods.

## Mandatory Reports

Health care providers are required by law to report any adverse event listed by the vaccine manufacturer as a contraindication to further doses, and any event listed in the national reportable events table that occurs within the specified time period after vaccination. Administration errors, such as giving the wrong vaccine, a wrong dose or a vaccine stored outside the recommended temperature range, should also be reported even when no adverse event occurs.

## How to Report

Reports are submitted online or with a downloadable form. Include patient information, the vaccines given with lot numbers and sites of administration, the date and time of vaccination and of adverse event onset, a description of the event, relevant medical history and the outcome. Reports of serious events, such as death, life-threatening illness or hospitalization, should be submitted as soon as possible, and follow-up information is requested on serious reports.

## Observation and Syncope Prevention

Vaccine providers should observe patients for 15 minutes after vaccination, seated or lying down, to reduce injury from syncope, which is common in adolescents and young adults. Persons with a history of an immediate allergic reaction of any severity to a previous dose, or with a history of anaphylaxis due to any cause, are observed for 30 minutes.

## Managing Anaphylaxis

Anaphylaxis is a rare but life-threatening reaction that usually begins within minutes of vaccination. All vaccination sites must have epinephrine available, staff trained to recognize the signs, and a protocol for calling emergency medical services. Every case of anaphylaxis after vaccination must be reported.
