# Outbreak Investigation Checklist for Infection Preventionists

## Cluster or Outbreak

A cluster is a group of infections or colonizations close together in time or place, possibly more than expected. An outbreak is an increase above the expected baseline that is confirmed after assessment. Every cluster gets a prompt initial review; a single case of a pathogen of high concern can also start a response.

## Initial Assessment

Rule out pseudo-outbreaks from laboratory error, specimen contamination or new testing methods. Compare the number of cases with the usual baseline for the unit and season. Notify facility leadership and, when required, the state or local health department.

## Case Definition and Line List

Write a working case definition with clinical or laboratory criteria, place and time period. Keep a line list with one row per case: identifiers, admission and symptom onset dates, room and unit history, procedures, devices, exposures, laboratory results and case status. Use it to draw an epidemic curve.

## Act Without Waiting

Start control measures based on the likely route of transmission without waiting for laboratory confirmation or typing results. Observe hand hygiene, PPE use, injection practices and cleaning in the affected area.

## Follow-Up

Continue surveillance until no new cases occur for twice the maximum incubation period, document the investigation and corrective actions in a final report, and share lessons learned with staff.
