# Hand Hygiene Recommendations for Health Care Personnel

## When to Clean Hands

Health care personnel (HCP) must clean their hands immediately before touching a patient; before performing an aseptic task such as placing an indwelling device or handling an invasive device; before moving from a contaminated body site to a clean body site on the same patient; after touching a patient or the patient's immediate environment; after contact with blood, body fluids or contaminated surfaces; and immediately after removing gloves. These indications align with the internationally used five moments for hand hygiene.

## Choosing Between Handrub and Soap

Alcohol-based hand sanitizer containing at least 60% alcohol is the preferred method for cleaning hands in most clinical situations, because it reduces the number of germs more effectively than soap and is less drying to skin. Use soap and water when hands are visibly soiled, before eating, after using the restroom, and when caring for patients with known or suspected infectious diarrhea, particularly Clostridioides difficile or norovirus, because alcohol does not reliably kill these organisms.

## Technique

When using alcohol-based hand sanitizer, put the product on the palm of one hand, using enough to cover all surfaces, and rub hands together until they feel dry, which takes about 20 seconds. Do not wipe the product off before it has dried. When washing with soap and water, wet hands first, apply soap, and rub hands together vigorously for at least 15 seconds covering all surfaces, then rinse, dry with a disposable towel and use the towel to turn off the faucet. Avoid hot water, which increases the risk of dermatitis.

## Gloves and Hand Hygiene

Wearing gloves does not replace the need for hand hygiene. Change gloves and clean hands when moving from a contaminated body site to a clean body site, between patients, and when gloves are damaged. Do not wash or sanitize gloves for reuse.

## Patient and Visitor Engagement

Facilities should provide hand sanitizer at the entrance to patient rooms and at points of care, and should encourage patients and visitors to clean their hands and to remind HCP to do the same. Educational material should be available in the languages commonly spoken by patients.
