# Vaccine Safety Quick Reference for Immunization Clinics

## Before Vaccinating

Screen every patient for contraindications and precautions, including a history of severe allergic reaction to a previous dose or to a vaccine component. Tell patients and parents which mild reactions to expect, such as a sore arm, low-grade fever or tiredness, and when to seek care. Confirm that emergency supplies are in date before the clinic opens.

## Observation Period

Keep every patient seated or lying down and under observation for 15 minutes after vaccination, because fainting is common, especially in adolescents and young adults, and can cause head injury. Extend observation to 30 minutes for anyone with a history of an immediate allergic reaction of any severity to a vaccine, or with a history of anaphylaxis from any cause.

## Anaphylaxis Readiness

Anaphylaxis is rare, but it can begin within minutes. Every clinic must have epinephrine on site, along with supplies to give it, staff trained to recognize anaphylaxis, and a written protocol for calling emergency medical services. Give intramuscular epinephrine without delay when anaphylaxis is suspected, then call for emergency help. Report every case of anaphylaxis after vaccination.

## What to Report

Report any clinically significant adverse event after vaccination, even if you are not sure the vaccine caused it. Reporting is required for events listed in the national reportable events table and for events the manufacturer lists as contraindications to further doses. Also report vaccine administration errors, such as the wrong vaccine, the wrong dose or a vaccine stored outside the recommended temperature range, even when the patient had no adverse event.

## How and When to Report

Submit reports online or on the standard form. Serious events, such as death, life-threatening illness, hospitalization or permanent disability, are reported as soon as possible. A complete report includes the patient's details, the vaccines given with their lot numbers, sites and routes of administration, the date and time of vaccination and of onset, a description of the event, relevant medical history and the outcome.
