# Handling Regulated Medical Waste

## What Counts as Regulated Medical Waste

Regulated medical waste, sometimes called infectious waste, includes microbiology laboratory waste such as cultures and stocks, pathology and anatomy waste, blood specimens and blood products, and sharps. Most other waste generated in patient care, such as soiled dressings that are not saturated with blood, can be handled as general waste under most state regulations. Over-classification of waste as regulated medical waste increases costs and environmental impact without improving safety.

## Containers and Labeling

Place regulated medical waste in red bags or containers labeled with the biohazard symbol that are leak-resistant and closable. Sharps go into closable, puncture-resistant, leak-proof sharps containers that are labeled or color-coded. Replace sharps containers routinely and do not allow them to overfill; many facilities set the fill line at three-quarters full. Close containers before removal to prevent spillage during handling and transport.

## On-Site Storage and Transport

Store regulated medical waste in a secure area with limited access, protected from weather and pests, and with surfaces that can be easily cleaned. Transport waste within the facility in closed carts on routes that avoid patient care and food service areas where possible. Off-site transport is performed by licensed haulers with a tracking manifest.

## Treatment Methods

Regulated medical waste is treated before disposal by steam sterilization, incineration or an alternative technology approved by the state. Liquid blood and body fluids may be carefully poured into a sanitary sewer connected to a treatment facility, unless local rules prohibit it, while wearing appropriate PPE.

## Spill Management

Clean spills of blood or body fluids promptly. Wear gloves and other PPE as needed, absorb the material with disposable towels, clean the area, and then disinfect it with an EPA-registered disinfectant or a fresh dilution of household bleach, observing the contact time. Discard contaminated materials as regulated medical waste.
