# Isolation Precautions for Preventing Transmission of Infectious Agents

## Two Tiers of Precautions

Isolation precautions are organized in two tiers. Standard precautions apply to the care of all patients in all health care settings, regardless of suspected or confirmed infection status. Transmission-based precautions are used in addition to standard precautions for patients with documented or suspected infection or colonization with pathogens that are transmitted by contact, droplet or airborne routes. Empiric precautions are started on the basis of the clinical syndrome while the diagnosis is pending, for example contact precautions for acute diarrhea of likely infectious origin.

## Contact Precautions

Place the patient in a single-patient room when available. When single rooms are scarce, prioritize patients with conditions that may facilitate transmission, such as uncontained drainage or stool incontinence. HCP wear a gown and gloves for all interactions that may involve contact with the patient or the patient's environment, donning them on room entry and discarding them before exiting. Use disposable or dedicated equipment such as blood pressure cuffs.

## Droplet Precautions

Place the patient in a single room when available. If patients are placed in multi-patient rooms, keep spatial separation of at least 3 feet (about 1 metre) and draw the privacy curtain between beds. HCP don a mask upon entry into the patient room or space. Patients on droplet precautions who must be transported outside the room wear a mask and follow respiratory hygiene and cough etiquette.

## Airborne Precautions

Place the patient in an airborne infection isolation room (AIIR) that has negative pressure relative to surrounding areas, a minimum of 12 air changes per hour for new construction and renovation, and air exhausted directly to the outside or recirculated through HEPA filtration. Keep the door closed. HCP wear a fit-tested N95 or higher-level respirator for respiratory protection when entering the room of a patient with known or suspected infectious pulmonary or laryngeal tuberculosis, measles or varicella. Restrict susceptible HCP from entering rooms of patients with measles or varicella when immune staff are available.

## Patient Transport and Visitors

Limit transport of patients on transmission-based precautions to medically necessary purposes. Notify the receiving area in advance, cover infected or colonized areas of the patient's body, and have the patient wear a mask for droplet and airborne precautions. Visitors are educated about the precautions in use and offered appropriate PPE.
