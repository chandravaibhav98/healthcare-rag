# Pocket Guide to Cleaning and Disinfecting Patient Areas

## Clean First, Then Disinfect

Always remove visible dirt and organic matter with detergent and water before applying a disinfectant. Blood, body fluids and dust protect microorganisms and inactivate most disinfectants, so disinfecting a dirty surface does not work.

## Contact Time

Use only facility-approved disinfectants at the correct dilution. The surface must stay visibly wet for the full contact time on the product label; if it dries early, apply more product. Prepare dilutions fresh every day unless the label says otherwise.

## How Often to Clean

Clean and disinfect high-touch surfaces, such as bed rails, call bells, light switches, door handles and monitor controls, at least once a day in general wards and at least twice a day in intensive care and isolation rooms. Always work from clean areas to dirty areas and from high surfaces to low surfaces.

## Special Situations

For patients with Clostridioides difficile, use a sporicidal product, such as a chlorine-releasing solution at 1,000 ppm available chlorine. After a patient is discharged or transferred, or when transmission-based precautions end, perform a terminal clean of all surfaces and equipment, change the curtains, and remove linen and waste before the room is used again.

## Checking Quality

Check cleaning with visual inspection, fluorescent markers or ATP testing, share the results with cleaning staff and supervisors, and retrain staff when failures are repeated.
