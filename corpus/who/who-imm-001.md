# Immunization of Health Workers

## Rationale

Vaccinating health workers protects them from occupational infection, protects vulnerable patients from transmission and maintains workforce capacity during outbreaks. Facilities should have a written policy describing which vaccines are offered, how immunity is documented and how unvaccinated staff are managed. Vaccines should be offered free of charge during working hours.

## Recommended Vaccines

The following vaccines are recommended for health workers who have no documented immunity:

- Hepatitis B: three doses at 0, 1 and 6 months for workers exposed to blood or body fluids.
- Influenza: one dose every year, before the start of the influenza season.
- Measles, mumps and rubella: two doses at least 28 days apart.
- Varicella: two doses 4–8 weeks apart for workers without evidence of immunity.
- Tetanus, diphtheria and pertussis: one dose of pertussis-containing vaccine, then tetanus-diphtheria boosters every 10 years.
- COVID-19: according to the current national schedule.

## Hepatitis B Post-Vaccination Testing

Workers at ongoing risk of exposure to blood should have their antibody to hepatitis B surface antigen (anti-HBs) measured 1–2 months after completing the primary series. A concentration of at least 10 mIU/mL indicates protection. Workers who do not respond receive a second complete series and are retested; persistent non-responders are counselled about their risk and the need for prompt post-exposure management.

## Documentation and Records

Occupational health keeps a confidential record of each worker's vaccination dates, serology results and documented declinations. Acceptable evidence of immunity includes written vaccination records, laboratory evidence of immunity or a documented history of disease where national policy allows. Records are reviewed at hiring and annually thereafter, and reminders are issued when doses are due.

## Declination and Work Restrictions

Workers who decline a recommended vaccine are informed of the risks and sign a declination form. During outbreaks, non-immune workers may be excluded from caring for affected patients, or excluded from work for the incubation period after an exposure, in line with the outbreak control plan.
