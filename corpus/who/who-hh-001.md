# Hand Hygiene in Health Care Settings

## Purpose and Scope

This guideline sets out when and how health workers must perform hand hygiene in any facility where patient care is delivered. It applies to all staff who have direct or indirect contact with patients or the patient zone, including physicians, nurses, allied health professionals, students, cleaners and porters. Hand hygiene is the single most effective measure for preventing the transmission of health care-associated infections and antimicrobial-resistant organisms. Hands become contaminated through contact with patients, medical devices and surfaces in the patient zone, and they transfer microorganisms to the next patient or object touched. Gloves reduce but do not eliminate this risk, so hand hygiene remains required when gloves are worn. Facilities are expected to provide the infrastructure, training and monitoring described in this document and to treat hand hygiene compliance as a patient-safety indicator reported to senior management.

## The Five Moments for Hand Hygiene

Health workers must perform hand hygiene at five moments during care:

1. Before touching a patient, to protect the patient from organisms carried on the hands.
2. Before a clean or aseptic procedure, such as inserting an invasive device, handling a wound dressing or preparing medication.
3. After a body fluid exposure risk, including after glove removal following such a task.
4. After touching a patient, at the end of the care episode or when leaving the patient side.
5. After touching patient surroundings, such as the bed rail, monitor or bedside table, even if the patient was not touched.

A single action can satisfy two moments when they occur together, for example moving from one patient to the next. The moments apply at the point of care, which is the place where the patient, the health worker and the care activity come together.

## Technique and Duration

Alcohol-based handrub is the preferred method for routine hand hygiene because it is faster, more effective against most organisms and better tolerated by skin than soap and water. Apply a palmful of product to cover all surfaces of both hands and rub until dry; the full procedure takes 20–30 seconds. Handrub formulations should contain 60–80% alcohol.

Wash hands with soap and water when they are visibly soiled, after using the toilet, and when exposure to spore-forming organisms such as Clostridioides difficile is suspected or confirmed. Wet hands, apply soap, rub all surfaces including the backs of the hands, between the fingers and the thumbs, rinse and dry thoroughly with a single-use towel; the full procedure takes 40–60 seconds. Do not use handrub and soap at the same time.

## Skin Care and Personal Practices

Healthy skin is essential because damaged skin harbours more organisms and discourages compliance. Facilities must provide compatible hand lotions and assess staff who report dermatitis. Fingernails must be kept short. Artificial nails, nail extensions and gel polish must not be worn by staff who have direct patient contact. Rings, bracelets and wrist watches should be removed before hand hygiene; a plain band may be permitted by local policy. Sleeves should be above the elbow during care activities.

## Monitoring and Feedback

Compliance is measured by trained observers using direct observation of hand hygiene opportunities against the five moments. Observation results, product consumption and infrastructure audits are combined to give a complete picture. Results are fed back to ward teams and managers regularly, and units with low compliance develop an improvement plan with clear actions and a review date. Observers must be validated before collecting data so that results are comparable between units and over time.
