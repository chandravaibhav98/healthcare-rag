# Prevention of Device-Associated Infections

## Overview

Invasive devices such as central venous catheters, urinary catheters and mechanical ventilators bypass the body's natural defences and are among the most important causes of health care-associated infection. The risk rises with every day a device remains in place. Prevention relies on avoiding unnecessary devices, inserting them aseptically, maintaining them correctly and removing them as soon as they are no longer needed. Facilities implement these measures as care bundles: small sets of evidence-based practices that are performed together and audited for full compliance.

## Central Line Insertion Bundle

1. Perform hand hygiene before insertion.
2. Use maximal sterile barrier precautions: cap, mask, sterile gown, sterile gloves and a full-body sterile drape.
3. Prepare the skin with an alcoholic chlorhexidine solution containing more than 0.5% chlorhexidine and allow it to dry.
4. Avoid the femoral site in adults when another site is suitable.
5. Use ultrasound guidance where available to reduce insertion attempts.

## Central Line Maintenance

Perform hand hygiene and disinfect needleless connectors and hubs by scrubbing before each access. Replace transparent dressings at least every 7 days and gauze dressings every 2 days, or sooner if damp, loosened or soiled. Review the need for the line every day and remove it promptly when it is no longer essential.

## Urinary Catheter Bundle

Insert urinary catheters only for accepted indications, such as acute urinary retention or accurate output monitoring in critically ill patients, and never for staff convenience. Insert them aseptically using sterile equipment. Maintain a closed drainage system, keep the collection bag below the level of the bladder and off the floor, and ensure unobstructed urine flow. Review the need for the catheter daily and remove it as soon as possible; nurse-led removal protocols reduce catheter days.

## Surveillance and Feedback

Device-associated infection rates are expressed per 1,000 device days so that units of different sizes can be compared. Rates and bundle compliance are fed back monthly to the clinical teams, and every infection is reviewed to identify missed opportunities for prevention.
