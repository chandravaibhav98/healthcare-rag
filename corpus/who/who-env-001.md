# Environmental Cleaning in Health Care Facilities

## Why Environmental Cleaning Matters

Contaminated surfaces in the patient zone act as reservoirs from which pathogens are transferred to the hands of health workers and to patients. Organisms such as methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus, vancomycin-resistant enterococci and Clostridioides difficile spores can survive on dry surfaces for weeks to months. Effective cleaning physically removes organic material and microorganisms, and disinfection then kills remaining organisms. Cleaning must always come before disinfection, because organic matter inactivates most disinfectants. Environmental cleaning is therefore a core element of standard precautions and of every outbreak control plan.

## Risk-Based Cleaning Frequencies

Cleaning frequency is set according to the risk of contamination and the vulnerability of patients. High-touch surfaces, such as bed rails, call bells, light switches, door handles and monitor controls, are cleaned and disinfected at least once daily in general wards and at least twice daily in high-risk areas such as intensive care and isolation rooms. Low-touch surfaces, such as walls and windows, are cleaned on a scheduled basis and when visibly soiled. Patient-care areas are cleaned from the cleanest to the dirtiest areas and from high to low surfaces, so that dirt is not spread to areas already cleaned.

## Terminal Cleaning

Terminal cleaning is performed after the discharge or transfer of a patient, and at the end of transmission-based precautions. All surfaces and equipment in the room are cleaned and disinfected, curtains are changed and linen and waste are removed. For rooms of patients with Clostridioides difficile, a sporicidal agent such as a chlorine-releasing solution at 1,000 ppm available chlorine is used. The room is not used for a new patient until terminal cleaning is complete and has been checked.

## Products and Contact Time

Use only disinfectants approved by the facility, prepared at the correct dilution and applied for the full contact time stated on the label. A surface must remain visibly wet for the whole contact time to achieve disinfection. Prepare dilutions fresh each day unless the manufacturer states otherwise, and label containers with the product name, concentration and date of preparation. Cleaning cloths and mop heads are changed between rooms and laundered or discarded after use.

## Monitoring Cleaning Quality

Cleaning quality is monitored through visual inspection, fluorescent marker audits or adenosine triphosphate testing. Results are shared with environmental services staff and their supervisors, and repeated failures lead to retraining.
