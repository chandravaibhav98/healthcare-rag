# Pocket Guide to Hand Hygiene at the Point of Care

## Your Five Moments

Clean your hands before touching a patient, before a clean or aseptic procedure, after a body fluid exposure risk, after touching a patient, and after touching the patient's surroundings. Hand hygiene is done at the point of care, where the patient, the health worker and the care activity come together.

## Handrub or Soap and Water

Alcohol-based handrub with 60–80% alcohol is the first choice for routine hand hygiene. Use soap and water instead when hands are visibly dirty, after using the toilet, and when Clostridioides difficile or another spore-forming organism is suspected or confirmed. Do not use handrub and soap at the same time.

## How Long It Takes

Apply a palmful of handrub, cover all surfaces of both hands, and rub until dry: 20–30 seconds in total. Handwashing with soap and water, including wetting, rubbing every surface, rinsing and drying with a single-use towel, takes 40–60 seconds.

## Gloves

Gloves do not replace hand hygiene. Clean your hands before putting gloves on when an aseptic task follows, and always immediately after removing them. Change gloves between patients and never wash or reuse single-use gloves.

## Nails, Jewellery and Skin

Keep fingernails short. Do not wear artificial nails, extensions or gel polish when caring for patients. Remove rings, bracelets and watches before hand hygiene, and use the hand lotion provided to keep skin healthy.
