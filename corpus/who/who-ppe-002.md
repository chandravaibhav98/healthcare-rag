# Personal Protective Equipment Selection and Use (Revised)

## What Changed in This Revision

This revision replaces the 2019 edition. The main changes are: respirator fit testing is now required annually instead of every two years; the gown and gloves are removed together as one step during doffing; and a trained observer or checklist is recommended when removing PPE after care of patients with high-consequence infectious diseases. Facilities should update local procedures and training materials within six months of publication.

## Principles of PPE Selection

PPE protects health workers from exposure to blood, body fluids and infectious agents, and it is the last line of defence in the hierarchy of controls. Select PPE on the basis of a point-of-care risk assessment that considers the task, the expected route of transmission and the volume of body fluid. Gloves are worn for contact with blood, body fluids, mucous membranes and non-intact skin. Gowns are worn when clothing or skin may be soiled; fluid-resistant gowns are used when large volumes are expected. A medical mask and eye protection are worn when splashes or sprays are likely. A particulate respirator such as an N95 or FFP2 is worn for aerosol-generating procedures and for airborne pathogens.

## Respirator Fit Testing and Seal Checks

Every health worker who may need a particulate respirator must pass a fit test before first use and annually thereafter. A new fit test is also required when the worker changes respirator make, model or size, or after significant weight change, dental work or facial surgery. A user seal check must be performed every time a respirator is put on; a failed seal check means the respirator must be readjusted or replaced before entering the patient area. Facial hair under the seal prevents an adequate fit.

## Donning Sequence

1. Perform hand hygiene.
2. Put on the gown and fasten it at the neck and waist.
3. Put on the medical mask or respirator and perform a user seal check.
4. Put on eye protection (goggles or face shield).
5. Put on gloves, pulling them over the gown cuffs.

Check the complete ensemble, ideally with a colleague, before entering the patient area.

## Doffing Sequence

1. Remove the gown and gloves together: grasp the gown at the front, pull it away from the body and roll it inside out, peeling the gloves off with it.
2. Perform hand hygiene.
3. Leave the patient room or anteroom if a respirator is worn.
4. Remove eye protection by the headband or arms.
5. Remove the mask or respirator by the ties or elastics without touching the front.
6. Perform hand hygiene.

Dispose of single-use items as infectious waste. Reusable eye protection must be cleaned and disinfected before reuse.
