# Decontamination and Reprocessing of Medical Devices

## The Spaulding Classification

Reusable medical devices are reprocessed according to the risk of infection associated with their use. Critical items enter sterile tissue or the vascular system, such as surgical instruments and implants, and must be sterilized. Semi-critical items contact mucous membranes or non-intact skin, such as endoscopes and laryngoscope blades, and require at least high-level disinfection; sterilization is preferred where the device tolerates it. Non-critical items contact only intact skin, such as blood pressure cuffs and stethoscopes, and require cleaning followed by low-level disinfection. Single-use devices must not be reprocessed.

## The Decontamination Cycle

Reprocessing follows a defined sequence: point-of-use pre-cleaning, safe transport in closed containers, cleaning, inspection, packaging, sterilization or disinfection, storage and distribution. Instruments must be kept moist after use, because dried blood and tissue are very difficult to remove. Cleaning is the most important step: a device that has not been cleaned cannot be reliably disinfected or sterilized. Automated washer-disinfectors are preferred to manual cleaning because they are more reproducible and reduce staff exposure to sharps.

## Steam Sterilization Parameters

Steam under pressure is the preferred method for heat-tolerant items. Typical validated cycles are 121 °C for at least 15 minutes holding time in gravity-displacement sterilizers, or 134 °C for at least 3 minutes holding time in pre-vacuum sterilizers. Total cycle time is longer because it includes air removal, heating and drying. Packs must be dry before removal from the sterilizer; wet packs are considered non-sterile. Heat-sensitive devices are processed by low-temperature methods such as hydrogen peroxide gas plasma according to the manufacturer's instructions.

## Monitoring Sterilization

Each cycle is monitored with physical indicators (time, temperature and pressure records), chemical indicators on every pack, and biological indicators at a frequency set by national policy and at least weekly. An air-removal test such as the Bowie-Dick test is performed each day before the first load in pre-vacuum sterilizers. Loads containing implants are released only after a negative biological indicator result, except in documented emergencies. Records must allow every pack to be traced to the patient on whom it was used.

## Storage of Sterile Items

Sterile packs are stored in a clean, dry, enclosed area with controlled temperature and humidity, at least 25 cm above the floor. Sterility is event-related rather than time-related: a pack remains sterile unless the packaging is damaged, wet or opened. Inspect packs before use and reprocess any that are compromised.
