# Pocket Guide to Managing Ill and Exposed Health Workers

## Health Assessment Before Starting Work

New health workers have a confidential pre-placement assessment with occupational health before starting work. It covers vaccination status and evidence of immunity, tuberculosis screening, fitness to wear a respirator where needed, and any adjustments required for the job.

## Tuberculosis Screening

At hiring, workers complete a symptom questionnaire and a risk assessment and are tested for latent tuberculosis infection with an interferon-gamma release assay or a tuberculin skin test. A positive test is followed by a chest X-ray and clinical review, and treatment for latent infection is offered.

## Working While Ill

Workers with fever, acute respiratory symptoms, vomiting, diarrhoea, draining skin lesions or conjunctivitis stay away from patient care. After a norovirus-like illness, return 48 hours after symptoms stop. After an influenza-like illness, return once fever has been absent for at least 24 hours without fever-reducing medicine.

## After Exposure to Measles or Varicella

Non-immune workers exposed to measles or varicella are excluded from work from the 5th day after their first exposure until the 21st day after their last exposure, and are offered post-exposure prophylaxis where indicated.
