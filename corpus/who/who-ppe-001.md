# Personal Protective Equipment Selection and Use

## Principles of PPE Selection

Personal protective equipment (PPE) protects health workers from exposure to blood, body fluids and infectious agents. PPE is the last line of defence in the hierarchy of controls and must be used together with engineering and administrative controls such as ventilation, triage and hand hygiene. Selection is based on a risk assessment of the anticipated exposure: the type of interaction with the patient, the likely route of transmission and the volume of body fluid involved. Gloves are worn for contact with blood, body fluids, mucous membranes and non-intact skin. Gowns or aprons are worn when clothing may be soiled. A medical mask and eye protection are worn when splashes or sprays are likely. A particulate respirator is worn for aerosol-generating procedures and for pathogens transmitted by the airborne route.

## Respirator Fit Testing

Health workers who may need a particulate respirator must be fit tested before first use with the specific make, model and size they will wear. Fit testing must be repeated at least every two years. A user seal check should be performed whenever the respirator is put on. Workers with facial hair under the sealing surface cannot achieve a reliable fit and must be offered an alternative such as a powered air-purifying respirator.

## Donning Sequence

Perform hand hygiene, then put on the gown, then the mask or respirator, then eye protection, and finally gloves. Gloves are pulled over the gown cuffs. Adjust the equipment before entering the patient area, because touching the face or adjusting PPE during care increases the risk of self-contamination.

## Doffing Sequence

Removal is the stage with the highest risk of self-contamination. Remove gloves first using a glove-to-glove and skin-to-skin technique. Perform hand hygiene. Remove the gown by pulling it away from the body and rolling it inside out. Perform hand hygiene. Remove eye protection by the headband or arms, then remove the mask or respirator by the ties or elastics without touching the front. Perform hand hygiene again. Respirators are removed after leaving the patient room. Discard single-use items in the appropriate waste stream.
