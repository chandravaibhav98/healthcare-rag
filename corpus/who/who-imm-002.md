# Pocket Guide to Vaccinating Health Workers

## Recommended Vaccines

Health workers without documented immunity are offered: hepatitis B, three doses at 0, 1 and 6 months for anyone exposed to blood; influenza, one dose every year before the season; measles, mumps and rubella, two doses at least 28 days apart; varicella, two doses 4–8 weeks apart; one dose of pertussis-containing vaccine followed by tetanus-diphtheria boosters every 10 years; and COVID-19 according to the national schedule.

## Checking Hepatitis B Protection

Measure antibody to hepatitis B surface antigen (anti-HBs) 1–2 months after the last dose of the primary series. A level of at least 10 mIU/mL means the worker is protected and needs no booster. A worker below this level receives a second complete series and is tested again; a worker who still does not respond is a non-responder and is counselled on prompt post-exposure management.

## Evidence of Immunity

Accept written vaccination records or laboratory evidence of immunity. A worker's own recollection of disease is accepted only where national policy allows it. Occupational health keeps the records confidential and reviews them at hiring and every year.

## Workers Who Decline

Workers who decline a recommended vaccine are told about the risks and sign a declination form. During an outbreak, non-immune workers may be kept away from affected patients or excluded from work for the incubation period after exposure.
