# Pocket Guide to Vaccine Storage at Health Facilities

## Correct Temperatures

Keep refrigerated vaccines between 2 °C and 8 °C at all times. Vaccines that must be frozen are kept between −50 °C and −15 °C, or as the manufacturer specifies. Never let freeze-sensitive vaccines, such as hepatitis B, tetanus toxoid-containing and other aluminium-adjuvanted vaccines, reach 0 °C, because freezing destroys them.

## Equipment and Packing the Refrigerator

Use a purpose-built vaccine refrigerator, not a domestic refrigerator with a freezer compartment. Place vaccines in their original boxes on the middle shelves, away from the walls, the floor and the cooling plate, with space for cold air to move. Never store food, drinks or specimens with vaccines. Put vaccines with the earliest expiry dates at the front so that they are used first.

## Checking Temperatures

Read and record the temperature at least twice every working day, in the morning and before closing, and check the minimum and maximum readings. Use an electronic data logger with an alarm where available. Any reading below 2 °C or above 8 °C is an excursion and requires immediate action.

## When the Temperature Goes Out of Range

Do not discard the vaccines and do not use them until a decision is made. Label them "do not use", keep them at the correct temperature, record how long the excursion lasted and the highest or lowest temperature reached, and ask the immunization programme or manufacturer for advice. If freezing is suspected, use the shake test only when the programme recommends it. Record the decision and what was done to prevent a repeat.

## Cold Chain Focal Person

Every facility names a trained cold chain focal person and a backup. They monitor temperatures, maintain the equipment and the stock, and lead the response to excursions and power cuts.
