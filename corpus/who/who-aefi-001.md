# Surveillance of Adverse Events Following Immunization

## Definitions

An adverse event following immunization (AEFI) is any untoward medical occurrence that follows immunization and that does not necessarily have a causal relationship with the vaccine. AEFIs include vaccine product-related reactions, vaccine quality defects, immunization errors, immunization anxiety-related reactions and coincidental events. A serious AEFI is one that results in death, is life-threatening, requires hospitalization or prolongs an existing hospitalization, results in persistent disability, or is a congenital anomaly.

## What to Report

Health workers should report any AEFI that is serious, that is unexpected, that occurs in a cluster, that is suspected to be caused by an immunization error, or that causes significant parental or community concern. Reports should be made even when the health worker is unsure whether the vaccine caused the event, because surveillance depends on detecting signals that no single clinician could see.

## Reporting Timelines

Serious AEFIs are reported to the immunization programme immediately and at the latest within 24 hours of the health worker becoming aware of the event. Non-serious AEFIs are reported through the routine reporting system, normally within one week. The report includes the patient's details, the vaccine name, manufacturer, lot number and expiry date, the date and time of vaccination and of onset, a description of the event and its outcome.

## Investigation

A serious AEFI, a cluster or an event of community concern is investigated, and the investigation should begin within 48 hours of the report. Investigators review the patient's clinical history, the vaccine storage and handling, the administration technique and whether other recipients of the same lot were affected. Causality assessment is carried out by a trained committee using a standard method.

## Observation After Vaccination and Anaphylaxis Readiness

Vaccine recipients are observed for at least 15 minutes after vaccination, and for 30 minutes when they have a history of anaphylaxis to any cause. Every vaccination site must have an anaphylaxis kit, a written protocol and staff trained to recognise and treat anaphylaxis promptly.
