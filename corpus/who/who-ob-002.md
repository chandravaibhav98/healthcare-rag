# Pocket Guide to the First Steps in a Suspected Outbreak

## Recognise It

Suspect an outbreak when there are more cases than expected in one place and time, or when two or more cases are linked, for example patients on the same ward at the same time. A single case of a very unusual or high-consequence organism, such as a carbapenemase producer never seen before in the facility, is handled as a possible outbreak.

## Tell the IPC Team

Notify the infection prevention and control (IPC) team as soon as you suspect an outbreak, and no later than 24 hours. The IPC team informs facility management and, where the law requires, the public health authority.

## Investigation Steps

1. Confirm the diagnosis and that an outbreak exists.
2. Agree a case definition based on person, place and time.
3. Find further cases and record them in a line list.
4. Describe the cases and draw an epidemic curve.
5. Form hypotheses about the source and route of transmission.
6. Put control measures in place and check that they work.
7. Communicate the findings and write a final report.

## Control Measures Start Immediately

Do not wait for the investigation or laboratory results before acting. Reinforce hand hygiene and standard precautions, isolate or cohort affected patients with dedicated staff, increase cleaning, restrict admissions to the ward if necessary, and exclude symptomatic staff.

## Ending the Outbreak

Declare the outbreak over when no new cases have occurred for twice the longest incubation period of the organism, or for a period set by the outbreak team for colonizing organisms, and present a final report to the infection control committee.
