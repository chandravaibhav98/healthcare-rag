# Outbreak Detection and Response in Health Care Facilities

## Recognising an Outbreak

An outbreak is suspected when there are more cases of an infection than expected in a defined place and time, or when two or more cases are epidemiologically linked, for example on the same ward with overlapping stays. A single case of a highly unusual or high-consequence pathogen, such as a carbapenemase-producing organism new to the facility, is also treated as a potential outbreak. Laboratory alerts, ward staff observations and routine surveillance data are the main sources of early detection.

## Immediate Notification

Clinical staff who suspect an outbreak must notify the infection prevention and control (IPC) team without delay and within 24 hours at the latest. The IPC team informs facility management and, where required by law, the public health authority. Early notification allows control measures to start before transmission spreads to other units.

## Outbreak Investigation Steps

1. Confirm the diagnosis and verify that an outbreak exists.
2. Write a case definition with clear criteria for person, place and time.
3. Search for additional cases and record them in a line list.
4. Describe the cases by time, place and person, including an epidemic curve.
5. Develop hypotheses about the source and mode of transmission.
6. Implement and evaluate control measures.
7. Communicate findings and prepare a final report.

Steps often run in parallel; control measures must not wait for the investigation to finish.

## Control Measures

Reinforce standard precautions and hand hygiene, place affected patients on the appropriate transmission-based precautions, and cohort patients and dedicated staff where single rooms are insufficient. Increase environmental cleaning, restrict admissions and transfers to the affected unit when necessary, and screen contacts if the organism can be carried asymptomatically. Staff with symptoms of the outbreak illness are excluded from work according to the occupational health policy.

## Declaring the End of an Outbreak

The outbreak is declared over when no new cases have occurred for a period of twice the maximum incubation period of the pathogen, or for a period defined by the outbreak management team for colonizing organisms. A final report describes the investigation, control measures, lessons learned and recommendations, and is presented to the facility infection control committee.
