# Pocket Guide to Health Care Waste for Ward Staff

## Know the Categories

About 85% of health care waste is general waste that is no more dangerous than household waste. The other 15% is hazardous: infectious waste, sharps, pathological waste, pharmaceutical, cytotoxic and chemical waste, and radioactive waste. Mixing hazardous waste into general waste makes the whole load hazardous.

## Use the Right Container

Segregate waste at the point where it is produced. General waste goes in a black bag. Infectious waste goes in a yellow bag or container marked with the biohazard symbol. Sharps go in a yellow puncture-resistant sharps container. Chemical and pharmaceutical waste goes in a brown container, and cytotoxic waste in a purple container or a labelled yellow container according to national rules.

## Filling and Closing

Fill bags and containers to no more than three-quarters of their capacity, then close them and label them with the ward and date. Never press down on waste or decant it from one container into another.

## Storage Time

Waste is collected from the ward at least once a day and kept in a locked central store. Infectious waste should not be stored for more than 48 hours in temperate climates or 24 hours in hot climates unless it is refrigerated.

## Treatment

Infectious waste is treated before final disposal by autoclaving, microwave treatment or incineration that meets emission standards. Open burning is not acceptable.
