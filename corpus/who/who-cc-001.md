# Vaccine Cold Chain Management

## The Cold Chain

The cold chain is the system of people, equipment and procedures that keeps vaccines within the recommended temperature range from manufacture to administration. Vaccines that are exposed to heat or freezing can lose potency permanently, and the loss is usually invisible. Administering a vaccine that has lost potency leaves the recipient unprotected and may require revaccination. Every facility that stores vaccines must designate a trained cold chain focal person and a backup.

## Storage Temperatures

Most vaccines must be stored refrigerated between 2 °C and 8 °C. Freeze-sensitive vaccines, including those containing aluminium adjuvants such as hepatitis B and tetanus toxoid-containing vaccines, are damaged by temperatures at or below 0 °C. Some vaccines must be stored frozen, typically between −50 °C and −15 °C, as specified by the manufacturer. Diluents are stored as directed and cooled to 2–8 °C before reconstitution.

| Vaccine group | Storage range |
| --- | --- |
| Most refrigerated vaccines | 2 °C to 8 °C |
| Frozen vaccines | −50 °C to −15 °C |
| Diluents before reconstitution | 2 °C to 8 °C |

## Equipment and Arrangement

Use purpose-built vaccine refrigerators with a calibrated temperature monitoring device. Domestic refrigerators with a freezer compartment are not recommended. Store vaccines in their original packaging on the middle shelves, away from walls, the floor and cooling vents, and leave space for air circulation. Do not store food or drinks in vaccine refrigerators. Rotate stock so that vaccines with the earliest expiry dates are used first.

## Temperature Monitoring

Record the temperature at least twice each working day, at the start and end of the day, and review the minimum and maximum readings. Electronic data loggers with alarms are recommended. Any reading outside 2–8 °C is a temperature excursion and must be acted on immediately.

## Responding to a Temperature Excursion

Do not discard affected vaccines and do not use them until a decision has been made. Mark them "do not use", keep them in the correct temperature range, record the duration and temperatures reached, and contact the immunization programme or manufacturer for advice. Use the shake test when freezing of a freeze-sensitive vaccine is suspected and the programme recommends it. Document the outcome and the corrective actions taken.
