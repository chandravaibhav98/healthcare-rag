# Sharps Injury Prevention and Post-Exposure Management

## Scope and Risk

Sharps injuries expose health workers to bloodborne pathogens, chiefly hepatitis B virus, hepatitis C virus and human immunodeficiency virus. Most injuries occur during or after use of a device, during disposal, or when needles are recapped. Prevention combines elimination of unnecessary injections, safety-engineered devices, safe work practices, training and accessible sharps containers. Every facility must have a written sharps injury prevention plan that is reviewed annually.

## Safe Work Practices

Never recap used needles. If recapping is unavoidable for a specific procedure, use a one-handed scoop technique or a recapping device. Do not bend, break or remove needles from syringes by hand. Dispose of sharps immediately after use, at the point of use, by the person who used them. Do not pass sharps from hand to hand; use a neutral zone such as a tray. Use safety-engineered devices with integrated sharps-injury protection wherever available, and activate the safety feature immediately after use.

## Sharps Containers

Sharps containers must be puncture-resistant, leak-proof on the sides and bottom, closable and clearly labelled with the biohazard symbol. Place containers within arm's reach of where sharps are used, at eye level and upright. Close and replace containers when they are three-quarters full; never overfill or press down on contents. Sealed containers are stored securely until collection as infectious sharps waste.

## Immediate Actions After Exposure

After a needlestick or sharps injury, let the wound bleed freely and wash it with soap and running water; do not squeeze or scrub the wound and do not apply bleach or other caustic agents. Flush splashes to the eyes or mouth with clean water or saline. Report the exposure immediately to the supervisor and to occupational health, because time-sensitive decisions must be taken.

## Post-Exposure Assessment and Prophylaxis

Occupational health assesses the type of exposure, the source patient's status where known and the worker's hepatitis B vaccination and immunity status. When HIV post-exposure prophylaxis is indicated, it should start as soon as possible, ideally within 2 hours and no later than 72 hours after exposure, and is taken for 28 days. Hepatitis B immunoglobulin and vaccination are offered to non-immune workers according to the protocol. Baseline and follow-up blood tests are arranged, and the worker receives counselling. Every exposure is recorded and reviewed to identify preventable causes.
