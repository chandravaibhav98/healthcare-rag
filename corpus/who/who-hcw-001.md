# Safe Management of Health Care Waste

## Waste Categories

About 85% of health care waste is general, non-hazardous waste comparable to domestic waste. The remaining 15% is hazardous and includes infectious waste, sharps, pathological waste, pharmaceutical waste, cytotoxic waste, chemical waste and radioactive waste. Correct classification at the point of generation is essential, because mixing hazardous with general waste turns the whole volume into hazardous waste and increases cost and risk.

## Segregation and Colour Coding

Waste is segregated at the point of generation by the person who produces it, into containers that are colour-coded and labelled:

| Waste category | Container |
| --- | --- |
| General non-hazardous waste | Black bag |
| Infectious waste | Yellow bag or container with biohazard symbol |
| Sharps | Yellow puncture-resistant sharps container |
| Chemical and pharmaceutical waste | Brown container |
| Cytotoxic waste | Purple container or labelled yellow container per national rules |

Bags and containers are filled to no more than three-quarters of their capacity and then closed and labelled with the source and date.

## Collection and Storage

Waste is collected at least daily from clinical areas, using dedicated trolleys that are cleaned regularly. Central storage areas are locked, protected from weather, animals and unauthorised access, and located away from food preparation areas. Infectious waste should not be stored for longer than 48 hours in temperate climates or 24 hours in hot climates unless refrigerated.

## Treatment and Disposal

Infectious waste is treated before final disposal by autoclaving, microwave treatment or incineration in a facility that meets emission standards. Open burning is not acceptable. Sharps are treated and disposed of in a way that prevents reuse and injury, for example by autoclaving followed by shredding or encapsulation. Pharmaceutical and chemical wastes are returned to the supplier or managed by licensed contractors.

## Training and Protection of Waste Handlers

Waste handlers receive training on segregation, handling and spill response, wear heavy-duty gloves, closed footwear and aprons, and are offered hepatitis B and tetanus vaccination. Injuries and exposures are reported and followed up by occupational health in the same way as clinical sharps injuries.
