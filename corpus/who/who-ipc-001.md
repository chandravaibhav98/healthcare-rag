# Core Components of Infection Prevention and Control Programmes

## Purpose

This document describes the minimum organisational components that every health care facility needs for an effective infection prevention and control (IPC) programme. An IPC programme protects patients, health workers and visitors from preventable infections and supports the containment of antimicrobial resistance. It requires leadership commitment, dedicated resources and integration with patient-safety and quality improvement structures.

## IPC Programme and Team

Each acute care facility must have an active IPC programme with a dedicated, trained IPC team. As a minimum, the facility should have one full-time IPC professional for every 250 beds, with higher ratios for facilities with intensive care units or complex patients. The programme has clear objectives, an annual work plan and a budget approved by senior management. An infection control committee with multidisciplinary membership oversees the programme.

## Guidelines, Education and Training

Facilities develop evidence-based IPC guidelines adapted to local conditions, covering at least standard and transmission-based precautions, aseptic techniques, device management and environmental cleaning. All health workers receive IPC training at induction and refresher training at least annually. Training uses participatory methods and includes practical assessment, for example of hand hygiene technique or PPE donning and doffing.

## Surveillance and Monitoring

Surveillance of health care-associated infections and antimicrobial resistance is used to guide action. Facilities monitor IPC practices such as hand hygiene compliance and bundle adherence, and feed results back to staff and managers. Monitoring and feedback form a continuous improvement cycle rather than an end in themselves.

## Workload, Staffing, Bed Occupancy and Environment

Bed occupancy should not exceed the standard capacity of the facility, with beds spaced at least 1 metre apart. Staffing levels are adequate for patient workload. Facilities provide safe water, sanitation, hand hygiene stations at every point of care, and adequate ventilation, waste management and supplies of PPE.
