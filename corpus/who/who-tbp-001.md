# Standard and Transmission-Based Precautions

## Standard Precautions

Standard precautions are applied to all patients at all times, regardless of their diagnosis or presumed infection status. They comprise hand hygiene, the use of PPE based on risk assessment, respiratory hygiene and cough etiquette, safe injection practices and sharps management, environmental cleaning, handling of linen and waste, patient placement, and the reprocessing of reusable equipment. Standard precautions assume that every person is potentially carrying a pathogen that could be transmitted. They form the foundation on which transmission-based precautions are added when a specific infection is known or suspected.

## Contact Precautions

Contact precautions are used for infections spread by direct or indirect contact, such as multidrug-resistant organisms and Clostridioides difficile. Place the patient in a single room where possible, or cohort patients with the same organism. Wear gloves and a gown on entry to the room and remove them before leaving. Use dedicated or disposable patient-care equipment; if equipment must be shared, clean and disinfect it between patients. Limit patient transport to medically necessary purposes.

## Droplet Precautions

Droplet precautions are used for pathogens carried in respiratory droplets that travel short distances, such as influenza and pertussis. Place the patient in a single room or maintain a spatial separation of at least 1 metre, preferably 2 metres, between beds with a curtain drawn. Wear a medical mask when working within close range of the patient. The patient should wear a medical mask during transport if tolerated.

## Airborne Precautions

Airborne precautions are used for pathogens that remain infectious over long distances in the air, such as tuberculosis, measles and varicella. Place the patient in an airborne infection isolation room with negative pressure relative to the corridor and at least 12 air changes per hour, with the door kept closed. Health workers must wear a fit-tested particulate respirator before entering. Where possible, only staff with documented immunity care for patients with measles or varicella. If an isolation room is unavailable, place the patient in a single room with the door closed and a mask on the patient, and transfer them as soon as feasible.

## Discontinuing Precautions

Transmission-based precautions are continued for the duration recommended for each pathogen, which may be until symptoms resolve, until a defined period of effective therapy has elapsed or until laboratory clearance. The decision to discontinue precautions is made with the infection prevention and control team and documented in the patient record.
