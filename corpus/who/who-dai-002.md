# Pocket Guide to Preventing Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia

## Why It Matters

Ventilator-associated pneumonia is a common and serious infection in intensive care. Every extra day on a ventilator increases the risk, so the main aim is to shorten mechanical ventilation and to prevent aspiration of contaminated secretions. Non-invasive ventilation is used for suitable patients to avoid intubation altogether.

## The Ventilator Bundle

1. Keep the head of the bed raised to 30–45 degrees unless this is contraindicated.
2. Interrupt sedation daily and assess readiness to breathe spontaneously.
3. Use endotracheal tubes with subglottic secretion drainage when ventilation is expected to last more than 48–72 hours.
4. Keep the endotracheal cuff pressure between 20 and 30 cm H2O.
5. Give oral care, including toothbrushing, at least twice a day.
6. Mobilize patients early.

## Handling the Ventilator Circuit

Do not change ventilator circuits routinely; change them only when they are visibly soiled or malfunctioning. Drain condensate away from the patient and avoid unnecessary disconnections. Perform hand hygiene and wear gloves before touching the airway or suctioning.

## Surveillance

Ventilator-associated events are counted per 1,000 ventilator days so that units can be compared, and bundle compliance is checked daily and fed back to the team each month.
