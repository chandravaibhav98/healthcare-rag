# Pocket Guide to AEFI Surveillance for Vaccinators

## Key Definitions

An adverse event following immunization (AEFI) is any untoward medical event after vaccination, whether or not the vaccine caused it. An AEFI is classified as serious if it leads to death, is life-threatening, requires admission to hospital or prolongs a hospital stay, causes persistent or significant disability, or is a congenital anomaly. Immunization errors, such as giving a wrong dose or using a vaccine that was not stored correctly, are also AEFIs.

## At the Vaccination Session

Observe all vaccinees for at least 15 minutes, and for 30 minutes if they have ever had anaphylaxis. Keep an anaphylaxis kit with adrenaline (epinephrine) at every session and know the treatment protocol. Explain common mild reactions, such as pain at the injection site or a mild fever, and tell caregivers to return if symptoms are severe or unexpected.

## Which Events to Report

Report every serious AEFI, every unexpected or unusual event, any cluster of events, any event that may be caused by an immunization error, and any event causing community concern. Report even if you are not sure the vaccine was the cause; surveillance depends on reports from many vaccinators.

## Reporting Deadlines

Notify serious AEFIs to the immunization programme immediately, and in any case within 24 hours of becoming aware of them, by the fastest available means. Send the written AEFI report form with the vaccine name, manufacturer, lot number and expiry date, the dates and times of vaccination and onset, a description of the event and its outcome. Non-serious events are reported through routine reports within one week.

## Investigation

Serious AEFIs, clusters and events of community concern are investigated by a trained team, starting within 48 hours of notification. The team examines the patient's history, the storage and handling of the vaccine, the injection technique and other recipients of the same lot. A causality assessment committee classifies each case.
